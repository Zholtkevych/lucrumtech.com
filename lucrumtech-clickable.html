Industrial automation

AMOS

The problem

Changing automation on a production line was slow and expensive, so the manufacturer effectively couldn't experiment. Every adjustment carried a changeover cost that made iteration uneconomic.

Manufacturing

The problem

What we owned

A Windows-based automation system that takes validated orders and translates product configurations, sizing specifications and machine parameters directly into machine-ready instructions.

Manual operator input was eliminated from the order path, errors fell, and production workflows ran faster.

The diagnosis principle

The visible problem is usually not the real problem.

A hiring problem is often an organisational design problem. A performance problem is often a leadership problem. A retention problem is often a management problem. A delivery problem is often a decision-making problem.

Most interventions fail because they treat the symptom that is loudest rather than the cause that is upstream. We start by finding which one you actually have.

Three ways companies get here

You'll recognise one of these.

01 — Something has broken

  • Releases keep slipping and nobody can say what ships next
  • The architecture won't take the load and the team is permanently firefighting
  • Key people are leaving and you don't know why yet
  • A management layer is the problem and nobody will say it out loud

02 — The company has stopped moving

  • Everyone is busy and nothing material ships
  • The same problems return every quarter in a different shape
  • You doubled headcount and execution got worse, not better
  • Every decision still routes back through you

03 — Scaling into enterprise

  • Enterprise customers are asking for things you don't have — security reviews, SLAs, audit trails
  • The way you worked at 20 people is breaking at 80
  • Procurement, compliance and legal are in every deal now, and nobody owns that
  • You need process without becoming the kind of company that is all process

If more than two of these are true at once, that isn't a set of problems. It's one problem you haven't found yet.

The argument

Not a bigger team. Not more process. The right system for the next stage.

A great product needs more than great technology. It needs the right leadership, the right capabilities, the right people, the right operating model.

The work can start before there is an organisation, when there is only an idea. Or when a business has outgrown the structure that got it here. In both cases the objective is the same: build the right organisation for the business you are creating.

From business model to organisation

The real question is rarely "who should we hire?"

It's: what organisation does this business need in order to work, grow and create value?

Business model → capabilities → organisation → leadership → team → operating model → launch → scale

Hiring

A placement that leaves in month three was never a placement.

Hiring runs on a defined process — intake through onboarding and probation follow-up, tracked in ATS with agreed response times at every stage. No black box, no candidate going quiet for three weeks.

Most interviews test whether someone can do the job. We test three things: capability, fit with how the team actually works, and whether this is somewhere they will still want to be in two years. What the interview surfaces then shapes the onboarding — so the first 90 days are built around the specific person, not a template.
